# Figure Legends

## Figure 1. pLIN strain-level cluster composition and group concordance.

Distribution of 3,073 unique strain-level pLIN codes (L6) across 28 groups (20 Gram-negative Inc groups + 4 Gram-positive rep type groups + 2 *A. baumannii* rep type groups + 2 *P. aeruginosa* rep type groups). Of all strain-level codes, 3,006 (97.8%) contained plasmids from a single group, while 67 codes (2.2%) contained members from two or more groups. No mixed codes were observed spanning Gram-negative and Gram-positive groups, confirming robust compositional separation. The largest mixed-group cluster (pLIN 1327, n = 869) spanned six Gram-negative Inc groups (IncF, IncFIB, IncFIBK, IncFII, IncHI1, IncN), representing a compositional convergence zone. The second-largest mixed cluster (pLIN 860, n = 142) spanned five Inc groups and carried a mean of 14.4 AMR genes per plasmid with 44.4% mcr colistin resistance carriage, representing a multidrug resistance hub. The four Gram-positive groups contributed 255 additional strain-level codes (repSA_large 72, repSA_small 52, repEF_conj 63, repEF_res 69).

## Figure 3. Clinically critical resistance determinants mapped to pLIN lineages.

## Figure 6. Hierarchical clustering resolution and Simpson's Index of Diversity across pLIN levels.

Cluster count and discriminatory power at each of the six pLIN thresholds. At L1 (d <= 0.150, ~85% ANI), 8,077 plasmids form distinct subfamilies (Gram-negative, Gram-positive, *Acinetobacter*, and *Pseudomonas*). Progressive resolution increases through L3 (9 clusters), L4 (44 subclusters), L5 (131 clone complexes), to L6 (3,073 strain-level codes). Simpson's D increases from near-zero at L1 to 0.985 at L6, demonstrating tuneable discriminatory power. The Gram-positive groups contribute 255 unique strain-level codes (repSA_large 72, repSA_small 52, repEF_conj 63, repEF_res 69). Gram-negative and Gram-positive groups are distinguished by colour.

## Figure 7. FastANI validation of cosine-to-ANI threshold mapping.

Scatterplot of cosine distance versus FastANI for 4,970 within-group plasmid pairs sampled from the 20 Gram-negative Inc groups. The overall Spearman correlation (rho = -0.348, P < 10^-141) and per-group correlations are annotated; 15/20 groups showed significant correlation (P < 0.05). Dashed vertical lines indicate the six pLIN distance thresholds; dashed horizontal lines indicate the corresponding ANI equivalents. At the strain-level threshold (d <= 0.001), the median ANI is 99.9%. The strongest per-group correlations are IncFIC (rho = -0.88), IncI2 (rho = -0.81), and ColE (rho = -0.72). Groups with non-significant correlations due to range restriction (narrow within-group ANI, >93%) are indicated. FastANI validation was performed on Gram-negative Inc groups only.

## Figure 8. pLIN computational pipeline overview.

Schematic of the complete pLIN workflow from FASTA input to epidemiological output. Core pipeline: Step 1: FASTA upload. Step 2: Tetranucleotide (4-mer) frequency vector computation (256 features). Step 3: Plasmid versus chromosome contig identification via multi-signal scoring (sequence length, cosine distance to nearest training vector, header keyword matching, Inc group classifier confidence; score >=10 classified as plasmid, <=-10 as chromosome, borderline defaults to plasmid). Step 4: KNN-based group classification of identified plasmid contigs (k = 5, cosine distance, distance-weighted; 28 groups). Step 5: Nearest-neighbour pLIN code assignment against the reference database. Quality assessment modules: Step 6: Assembly completeness scoring (composite 0--100). Step 7: Database coverage and novelty detection (traffic-light flagging). Step 8: Recombination detection (minimap2 mosaic analysis). Annotation modules: Step 9: AMRFinderPlus integration (optional). Step 10: MOB-suite mobility prediction (optional). Step 11: MGE boundary detection (IS elements, composite transposons). Validation modules: Step 12: Mash/FastANI ANI validation (optional). Step 13: minimap2 SNP sub-typing (optional). Discovery modules: Step 14: Novel group discovery (low-confidence clustering). Step 15: Evolutionary rate estimation (SNP regression). Step 16: Adaptive threshold calibration (bootstrap stability). Surveillance modules: Step 17: MinCED CRISPR host inference (optional). Step 18: Outbreak detection. Step 19: Temporal clustering. Step 20: Interactive results display and export.

## Figure 10. Comparative feature matrix of plasmid classification systems.

Visual comparison chart summarising Table 1 across the five criteria for comprehensive plasmid nomenclature (hierarchical levels, code permanence, reference-free operation, taxonomic scope, AMR integration). pLIN is the only system satisfying all five criteria simultaneously, and the only tool supporting both Gram-negative and Gram-positive plasmid classification. PlasmidFinder and pMLST provide stable codes but lack hierarchy and require databases. MOB-suite offers broad scope but lacks code permanence and hierarchy. COPLA provides semi-hierarchical classification but with limited assignability (41%) and mutable codes. mge-cluster is reference-free but produces flat, non-permanent clusters.

## Figure 11. Software architecture and modular design of the pLIN Streamlit application.

Diagram of the eight-layer application architecture. Layer 1 (Feature extraction): BioPython FASTA parsing, NumPy 4-mer counting. Layer 2 (Contig identification): multi-signal plasmid versus chromosome scoring using sequence length, cosine distance to nearest training vector, header keyword matching, and Inc group classifier confidence. Layer 3 (Classification): scikit-learn KNN classifier (28 groups), nearest-neighbour pLIN assignment. Layer 4 (Quality assessment): assembly completeness scoring, database coverage and novelty detection, recombination analysis. Layer 5 (AMR annotation): AMRFinderPlus nucleotide-mode detection with --plus flag; MGE boundary detection and composite transposon identification. Layer 6 (Validation): Mash ANI estimation, FastANI true ANI, minimap2 SNP sub-typing. Layer 7 (Discovery): novel group discovery, evolutionary rate estimation, adaptive threshold calibration with cluster stability assessment. Layer 8 (Surveillance): outbreak detection, temporal clustering, MOB-suite mobility, MinCED CRISPR host inference. External tools follow a graceful degradation model (auto-detected at runtime; modules disabled if unavailable).

## Figure 24. Plasmid versus chromosome contig identification: multi-signal scoring validation.

**(A)** Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for the four individual scoring signals and the combined multi-signal model on the 1,000-contig synthetic benchmark (500 plasmid, 500 chromosomal). Individual signal AUCs: sequence length scoring (0.94), cosine distance to nearest plasmid training vector (0.89), Inc group classifier confidence (0.82), FASTA header keyword matching (0.71). Combined four-signal model AUC = 0.99, demonstrating substantial complementarity among signals. **(B)** Composite score distributions for true plasmid contigs (blue) and true chromosomal contigs (red), with decision boundaries at +10 (plasmid threshold) and -10 (chromosome threshold) indicated by dashed vertical lines. The borderline zone (-10 to +10) contains 3.4% of contigs (21 plasmids, 13 chromosomal), all defaulting to "plasmid" classification under the conservative design rule. **(C)** Confusion matrix showing 493 true plasmid, 485 true chromosome, 15 false plasmid (chromosomal contigs misclassified as plasmid), and 7 false chromosome (plasmid contigs misclassified as chromosome). The 7 missed plasmids are individually annotated with their sequence lengths and nearest-neighbour distances, revealing that 5/7 were large plasmids (>200 kb) with chromosomal-like composition. The 15 false plasmids were predominantly small chromosomal fragments (<30 kb). **(D)** Validation on 200 real whole-genome assemblies from NCBI (847 chromosomal contigs, 312 plasmid contigs): 96.5% concordance with submitter annotations, with discordant cases classified by type (megaplasmid, chromid, ambiguous annotation).
