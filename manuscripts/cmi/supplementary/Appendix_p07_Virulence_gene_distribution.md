# Appendix p 7: Virulence gene distribution by Inc type

| Inc type | VIR+ (%) | Top virulence genes | Clinical implication |
|----------|----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| IncFII | 23.2% | traT (25.6%), spvB/spvD (25.3%), mltE (24.0%), iucA (23.3%) | Salmonella plasmid virulence (spv), iron acquisition (siderophores); enhanced invasive infection risk |
| IncX1 | 11.8% | hlyA (48.2%), fedA/fedF (47.0%) | Alpha-haemolysin (tissue damage), fimbrial adhesins (ETEC virulence); diarrhoeal disease severity |
| IncFIB | 14.4% | traT (31.2%), iutA (22.1%) | Serum resistance (traT), aerobactin iron uptake; bacteraemia persistence |
| IncN | 2.9% | iutA (18.8%), iucC (15.6%), traT (12.5%) | Low virulence carriage but high AMR; primarily resistance-dissemination vehicles |

The co-occurrence of AMR and virulence determinants on the same plasmid is clinically significant because antibiotic pressure can co-select for virulence: prescribing any antibiotic to which the plasmid carries resistance simultaneously selects for the virulence factors on the same replicon. IncFII plasmids, with both the highest virulence carriage (23.2%) and substantial AMR carriage (62.3%), represent the most concerning co-selection risk.
