## Figure 6: pLIN hierarchical structure

Dendrogram illustrating the six-level hierarchical clustering structure for a representative subset of plasmids. Horizontal dashed lines indicate the six distance thresholds: L1 (d<=0.150), L2 (d<=0.100), L3 (d<=0.050), L4 (d<=0.020), L5 (d<=0.010), and L6 (d<=0.001). ## Figure 7: pLIN analysis pipeline overview

Schematic of the pLIN computational pipeline. Input: FASTA file(s) of assembled sequences (plasmid, chromosomal, or mixed assemblies). Step 1: tetranucleotide (4-mer) frequency computation (256-dimensional vector per sequence). Step 1b: automatic plasmid contig identification using multi-signal scoring (sequence length, cosine distance to nearest plasmid training vector, FASTA header keyword matching, and Inc/rep classifier confidence); contigs classified as chromosomal are excluded from downstream pLIN assignment. Step 2: KNN classification (k=5, cosine distance, distance-weighted) for Inc/rep group prediction across 28 groups (20 Gram-negative Inc, 4 Gram-positive, 2 *Acinetobacter*, 2 *Pseudomonas*). Step 3: within-group single-linkage hierarchical clustering for pLIN code assignment at six thresholds. Step 4: AMRFinderPlus annotation for AMR, virulence, and stress-response genes. Step 5 (optional): automated outbreak detection with clinical risk stratification (CRITICAL/HIGH/MODERATE). Step 6 (optional): analytical modules for assembly completeness assessment, database coverage and novelty detection, recombination detection, novel group discovery, evolutionary rate estimation, adaptive threshold calibration, and MGE boundary detection. Output: integrated report with pLIN codes, Inc/rep assignments, AMR profiles, outbreak alerts, and analytical module results.

## Figure 8: pLIN hierarchical classification system

Conceptual illustration of the six-level pLIN code structure using pLIN 1.1.2.15.48.671 as an example. Each digit represents assignment at a progressively finer clustering threshold: L1 (superfamily) through L6 (strain/outbreak level). The figure demonstrates how a single plasmid is simultaneously classified at all six resolution levels, enabling multi-scale epidemiological investigation from broad plasmid family surveys to outbreak confirmation.

## Figure 9: Comparison of plasmid classification methods

Radar chart comparing pLIN with five existing plasmid classification approaches (PlasmidFinder, pMLST, MOB-suite, COPLA, mge-cluster) across seven clinical utility dimensions: discriminatory power, hierarchical resolution, nomenclature stability, AMR integration, outbreak detection, risk stratification, and reference-free operation. pLIN uniquely achieves high scores across all dimensions.

## Figure 12: Resolution comparison across plasmid classification methods

Alluvial flow diagram showing how 74 outbreak plasmids from 27 studies across 13 countries are resolved by six classification methods, ordered by increasing resolution. Ribbons are coloured by resistance mechanism (KPC, NDM, OXA-48, mcr, CTX-M, VIM, IMP). PlasmidFinder produces 11 flat Inc group labels with 100% coverage but no hierarchical structure; pMLST resolves 10 sequence types but 39% of plasmids lack a typing scheme; MOB-suite classifies 61% into 8 cluster IDs (39% unclassified, grey bands); COPLA/PTU classifies 57% into 6 PTU groups (43% unclassified); mge-cluster resolves 22 clusters with 100% coverage but produces no permanent codes (dashed borders). pLIN resolves all 74 plasmids into 42 unique hierarchical codes with 100% coverage and permanent nomenclature. Callout annotations highlight three key outbreak examples: Ho 2019 NDM (12/13 plasmids share pLIN 475 vs all labelled "IncX3" by PlasmidFinder), OXA-48 cross-country transmission (5/6 share pLIN 1688 across Turkey, Netherlands, and France), and mcr-1 backbone separation (pLIN 87 for IncI2 carriers vs pLIN 340 for IncX4 carriers).

## Figure 18: Plasmid versus chromosome contig classification

Overview of the multi-signal scoring system for automatic plasmid contig identification. **(A)** Schematic of the four-signal scoring architecture: sequence length scoring (left; contigs >1 Mb receive -50 points, >500 kb receive -30 points, <300 kb receive +20 points, <20 kb receive +10 points), cosine distance to the nearest plasmid training vector (centre-left; shorter distances indicate greater plasmid similarity), FASTA header keyword matching (centre-right; plasmid-associated and chromosome-associated keyword lists with corresponding positive and negative score contributions), and KNN classifier confidence (right; Inc/rep group assignment probability contributing to the composite score). Composite scores are summed and classified: score >=10 denotes plasmid, score <=-10 denotes chromosome, and borderline scores (-10 to +10) default to plasmid (conservative). **(B)** Validation on 1,200 mixed contigs (600 chromosomal, 600 plasmid). Violin plots show the distribution of composite scores for chromosomal contigs (median -38.2) and plasmid contigs (median +27.4), with clear separation at the decision boundaries. **(C)** Performance stratified by contig size: contigs >500 kb (predominantly chromosomal, >95% specificity), contigs 50--500 kb (mixed, 89.4% correct classification from combined signals), and contigs <50 kb (predominantly plasmid, >98% sensitivity). Stacked bar charts show the relative contribution of each signal to correct classification within each size category. **(D)** Application example: a mixed *Klebsiella pneumoniae* assembly containing one 5.2 Mb chromosomal contig and three plasmid contigs (180 kb, 85 kb, and 9 kb). The contig classifier correctly identifies and excludes the chromosomal contig (composite score -62) while retaining all three plasmid contigs (composite scores +34, +28, and +19) for downstream pLIN assignment.
